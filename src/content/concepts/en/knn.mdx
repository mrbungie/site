**k-NN** does not learn a global formula: it stores the data and, when a new case arrives, looks at its nearest neighbors and decides by majority.

## The idea

The decision depends on the immediate neighborhood. If positive examples dominate around the new point, the prediction will be positive; if negatives dominate, the opposite happens.

## What to look for

- With a small $k$, the model reacts strongly to local noise.
- With a large $k$, the boundary becomes smoother and loses detail.
- The scale of the variables matters: distance (usually Euclidean) decides everything, so it is a good idea to standardize.
- In high dimensions, distances lose discriminating power (the "curse of dimensionality").

## Applications

Useful for prototypes, local pattern recognition, and settings where an intuitive rule based on proximity is preferred over a complex parametric model. Its cost is at prediction time: every new case has to be compared with the whole sample.
