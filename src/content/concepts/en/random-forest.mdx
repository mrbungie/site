**Random Forest** combines many decision trees trained on different samples and different subsets of variables. Instead of relying on a single branch, it gathers many small voices and decides by majority.

## Key intuition

A single tree is unstable: a small change in the data can alter its structure a lot (high variance). The forest reduces this problem by averaging trees that differ from one another. To achieve this, each tree is trained with:

- a **bootstrap** sample of the rows (with replacement), and
- a random subset of variables at each split.

Averaging weakly correlated trees lowers the variance without increasing the bias much. In addition, the rows left out of each bootstrap sample (*out-of-bag*) serve as free validation.

## What to look for

- Each tree uses similar rules, but not identical ones.
- The final decision depends on the **aggregation**, not on a single tree.
- You gain robustness at the cost of losing a simple global explanation.

## Applications

A strong baseline for tabular classification, fraud detection, credit risk and diagnosis when you want good performance without much fine-tuning.
